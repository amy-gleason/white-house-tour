# 🏛️ West Wing Tour

## 🔰 ARRIVAL & SECURITY PROCESS

### What to Say:
- "You'll check in at the first gate at 17th & State."
- "You'll need to show a REAL ID or passport."
- "They might also ask for the last four digits of your Social Security Number or your birthdate — especially for children."
- "You'll need your ID again at the second security gate so keep it handy."

### 🗺️ Wayfinding:
➡️ Walk straight to the second security booth on West Executive Drive, then continue toward the employee entrance to the West Wing.

## 🏛️ FIRST DIVISION MONUMENT

### What to Say:
- To the right as you come through the first check in gate is the First Division Monument that was meant to honor the World War 1 brave soldiers.
- It has a winged victory statue on top of the column and flowers are planted next to it in the shape of a 1 to represent the first division.
- It was placed there to balance the Sherman statue on the other side of the treasury
- It was the first monument to honor the troops. Other monuments have followed for Korean War, Vietnam war, World War II and a newer World War I monument off 15th.

## 🏢 EISENHOWER BUILDING - PART 1

### What to Say:
- "The massive building beside us is the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, or EEOB. It houses a majority of offices for White House staff."
- "It was originally built for the State, War, and Navy Departments between 1871 and 1888 and opened in phases — with the State Department moving in first. It was called the SWAN building back then."
- "It was designed by the same architect who did the Treasury Building — Alfred Mullett — but in a completely different style: French Second Empire architecture."
- "It took 17 years to complete and was the largest office building in the world when it opened. It has nearly two miles of hallways."

## 🏢 EISENHOWER BUILDING - PART 2

### What to Say:
- "Public opinion was divided — some people loved it, but many hated it and called it 'the great monstrosity.'"
- "The War Department operated here from 1871 to 1947 and planned all U.S. military conflicts until it was split into the Army and Air Force."
- "In the 1950s, there was a push to tear it down, but Truman said it was 'the greatest monstrosity in America' and preserved it."
- "The War Department existed for 158 years, from 1789 to 1947. From 1871 to 1947, it operated out of this building and was responsible for planning all U.S. military conflict during that time."

## 🏛️ APPROACHING THE WEST WING

### As You Get Close to the Entrance:
- "The West Wing was built by Teddy Roosevelt in 1902 because the East Wing was too chaotic with his big family."
- "It was supposed to be temporary — just while he was president — but future presidents liked having work space separate from the residence."
- "Taft expanded it in 1909 and added the first Oval Office."
- "It caught fire on Christmas Eve in 1929 — President Hoover rebuilt it and added air conditioning and a basement."
- "FDR later expanded it again in 1934 to make it like it is today."

## 🛰️ SITUATION ROOM

### What to Say:
- "This is the Situation Room — or 'Sit Room' — and despite the name, it's actually a suite of rooms."
- "After the failed Bay of Pigs invasion, JFK wanted to be sure he could receive intel and securely communicate with American assets anywhere so he built the Situation Room."
- "It's run by the National Security Council (NSC) for monitoring and dealing with crises."
- "It includes three conference rooms with state-of-the-art video conferencing capabilities and totals 5,000 square feet."
- "The facility runs 24/7 to monitor activity and to handle global events."
- "Fun fact: it was originally the Truman Bowling Alley that is now at the Eisenhower Building!"

## 🏛️ CABINET ROOM - PART 1

### What to Say:
- "This is where the President meets with the Cabinet. You have probably seen it recently on TV as the President has been televising the Cabinet meetings."
- Ask, "Which chair do you think is the President's chair?" Answer - "The President's chair here and in other spaces in the West Wing is about two inches taller than the other chairs and is usually in the center of the table."
- "Seating is arranged based on when each department was established — State, Treasury, Defense, etc. The Cabinet members over the oldest departments sit closest to the President."
- "The Secretary of State's chair is positioned to the President's right, while the Secretary of Defense is on his left."

## 🏛️ CABINET ROOM - PART 2

### What to Say:
- "The backs of the chairs have name plates that show who sits there. If someone has served in multiple cabinet roles, they will have multiple name plates."
- "Originally constructed in 1902 as a part of President Roosevelt's renovation, the room's current Georgian Style was designed in 1934."
- "The mahogany center conference table was originally bought by President Richard Nixon."
- "Cabinet chairs can be purchased at the end of the Cabinet member's term."

### 🗺️ Wayfinding:
➡️ Continue forward and slightly left to enter the Oval Office.

## 🏛️ OVAL OFFICE - OVERVIEW

### What to Say:
- "This is the working office of the President."
- "It was first made to be in the Oval shape by Taft in 1909. It was modeled after the Blue Room in the East Wing because Taft thought it would help with room flow."
- "Presidents can customize pretty much all of the room — but three things are fixed: the American flag must be there, there must be a portrait of George Washington, and the desk must face away from the window to show that the leader of the free world isn't afraid to have his back to the window"
- "The rest of the room from the carpet, curtains, paintings, furniture - pretty much everything else can be changed by the president. The rug in here is Reagan's rug."

## 🏛️ OVAL OFFICE - RESOLUTE DESK

### The Resolute Desk:
- "The Resolute Desk, used by eight presidents, was a gift from Queen Victoria in 1880. The HMS Resolute was a British naval ship that became icebound in the Arctic. American whalers discovered and salvaged the ship, and the U.S. restored and returned it to Britain as a gesture of goodwill."
- "There's a nearly identical twin desk made from the same ship — it's in Buckingham Palace."
- "JFK was the first to use it in the Oval Office in 1961 and most presidents have used it since then. Before it was in the Oval Office, it was also used by other presidents in the residence or Eisenhower building"
- "There are many iconic pictures of the Resolute desk but the one with JFK's kids playing around the desk is one of the most well known."
- "FDR had the front of the desk closed in because he didn't want his wheelchair to be seen as he thought it made him appear weak."

## 🏛️ OVAL OFFICE - ROOM FEATURES

### Room Features:
- "The presidential coat of arms was updated by Truman in 1956 to show the eagle facing the olive branch — a symbol of peace."
- "The gold leaf on the walls and in the ceiling and the $100,000 upgrade was added during Trump's presidency — it replaced a simple white inlay."
- "In the ceiling, do you notice anything funny looking about the stars?" - Answer - "The stars are Italian stars and nobody noticed that they had too many points until it was finished. They thought they would just fix it on the next renovation, but you have to remove the ceiling to fix it so it has stayed."
- "There is a box with a button by the phone - each President can program it to what they want to receive. Some choose coffee, but President Trump has it call for Diet Coke."
- "The door to the right leads to the private dining room — famously tied to the Monica Lewinsky story."

## 🏛️ OVAL OFFICE - ARTIFACTS & FUN FACTS - PART 1

### Artifacts & Fun Facts:
- "The World Cup trophy nearby is worth over $20 million."
- "There's also a model of the new Air Force One on the table."
- "The clock on the mantel uses 'IIII' instead of 'IV' — this was something early Americans did to show their independence and to rebel from the roman numeral IV and show it as IIII."
- "Behind the curtain is a rare copy of the Declaration of Independence, added by Trump. It's kept behind the curtain to protect it from light. It is the third copy and the original is in the National Archives"

## 🏛️ OVAL OFFICE - ARTIFACTS & FUN FACTS - PART 2

### More Fun Facts:
- "The back of the door looks like the same wallpaper as the walls to make it look like the rest of the walls when the door is closed."
- "Trump used to drag the military flags in here from the Roosevelt Room for pictures — eventually they made replicas just for him to use in the Oval. We will talk more about those flags when we get to the Roosevelt Room."
- Outside the Oval, look up and ask, "Guess how much that eagle weighs?" - wait for answers and don't answer any probing questions. Answer - "It weighs about two pounds. Nancy Reagan was afraid that the original heavy one would fall on someone, so she had it replaced with this one that is made of lightweight wood and painted gold."

## 🏛️ ROOSEVELT ROOM

### What to Say:
- "This is a meeting room named in honor of both Roosevelts because Teddy created the first West Wing and Franklin made it into what it is today."
- "It was previously known as the 'Fish Room' because of various aquariums and fishing mementos that were in the room and on the wall."
- "President John F. Kennedy once hung a large mounted sailfish he had caught in Acapulco, Mexico in this room."
- "Presidents use this room for more informal or roundtable meetings."
- "There are portraits of both Roosevelts — the one over the fireplace changes based on the president's preference."
- "Teddy Roosevelt's Medal of Honor and Nobel Peace Prize are displayed here — sometimes easier to see from one side of the room than the other."

### 🗺️ Wayfinding:
➡️ Go back toward the Cabinet Room and turn left across from it. While walking in the hallway, mention the incredible art collection owned and displayed by the White House.

## 🏛️ WEST WING MAIN LOBBY - PART 1

### What to Say:

## 🏛️ WEST WING MAIN LOBBY - PART 2

### What to Say:

- "Across from it is a painting from the Battle of the Potomac (1861)."
- "Another painting shows ships from the War of 1812. One version of the story says the Americans mistook a British warship for an easy target — but it was fully armed and forced the U.S. ships to retreat.
