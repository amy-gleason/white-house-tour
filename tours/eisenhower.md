# 🏢 Eisenhower Building Tour

## ⚓ NAVY STEPS (OUTSIDE)

### What to Say:
- "As we approach the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, you'll notice the giant staircase in front of us — these are called the Navy Steps."
- "See the two big anchors at the top? That's a nod to this being the Navy side of the building."
- "Originally, this whole place was called the SWAN Building — for State, War, and Navy."
- "State is over to the right, War is on the other side, and we're standing on the Navy side."

### 💡 Photo Tip:
📸 Great photo spot with the White House in the background!

## 🏛️ ENTRYWAY LOBBY

### What to Say:
- "As you walk inside, look to your right. Every federal building has the official photos of the sitting President and Vice President — here you'll see President Trump and Vice President J.D. Vance."
- "To your left, you'll see a cabinet full of challenge coins. These started in the military to show what unit or troop someone was part of."
- "The running joke is that if you're at a bar and someone asks to see your challenge coin and you don't have one, you have to buy the drinks!"
- "This display has coins that have been given to President Trump since January 20th, and it's already full."

## 📚 INDIAN TREATY ROOM (4TH FLOOR)

### What to Say:
- "This room is labeled 'Library,' but it's known as the Indian Treaty Room. It used to be the Navy Law Library."
- "Nobody really knows why it's called the Indian Treaty Room — no treaty is believed to have been signed here."
- "It was the most expensive room to decorate and build in the entire building."
- "Ceremonial events are often held in this room."

### 🗺️ Guide Note:
Let folks take in the ornate ceilings, the marble, and the great view of the White House out the windows. Then, walk out of the room and go straight across the horizontal hallway to the other side.

## 🏢 GOLD DOME & CONFERENCE ROOM

### What to Say:
- "As we walk to the other side, you can see there are small doors in the hallways across the building. These were used to store coal when the building opened and now people like to hide surprises like gnomes in them."
- "You will see security boxes here. That is where you put your electronics when going into a secure room called a SCIF. People don't really put their things in the boxes and lock it. You will see phones and computers everywhere - it is a secure building and people are often in a hurry."
- "At the end of the hallway, look up at the gold dome which is really beautiful."
- "At the end of the hall here, there is a large conference room, just one example of where big meetings take place in this building."

### 🗺️ Guide Note:
When coming out of the conference room, turn left and take the elevator to the second floor. You will see Secretary of War Suite and you can go in at 236 or 234.

## ⚔️ SECRETARY OF WAR SUITE

### What to Say:
- "We're now on the War Department side. This hallway leads to the Secretary of War suite, from rooms 236 to 226."
- "This area was where all U.S. military operations were planned from 1871 to 1947."
- "Think of it like the U.S. version of Churchill's war bunker."
- "During times of conflict, men were locked in here to make critical decisions."
- "There was only one bathroom and one shower for the whole suite."

## 📜 ROOM 236

### What to Say:
- "Look for a xylographed (woodcut) copy of the U.S. Constitution on the wall — super rare."
- "Check out John Adams' hat box — sometimes it's in the cabinet, sometimes above the archway."
- "As you move through the rooms, admire the ornate ceilings, painted details, textured walls, and gorgeous floors."

## ⚔️ ROOM 230B

### What to Say:
- "Memorabilia includes a World War I helmet, mess kits, a bugle from 1870, and a Civil War cannonball."
- "The desk is original — with historic photos of what the room looked like during wartime."
- "President Taft's portrait is here — he's the only person to serve in all three branches of government: Congress, President, Secretary of War, and Chief Justice."

## 🏛️ ROOM 226

### What to Say:
- "More artifacts like flags with 38 and 48 stars, a period typewriter, and a Revolutionary War cannonball."
- "The room looks a lot like 236 but has different-colored ceiling gems."
- "As you walk, look up — there's another one of the building's domes here. And as you walk along the marble floors, keep an eye out for fossils in the stone!"

## 🌍 DIPLOMATIC RECEPTION ROOM

### What to Say:
- "This room was created as the first place foreign diplomats were welcomed when they came to the White House."
- "Look around — the chandeliers, fireplaces, carpet — it all looks nearly identical to photos taken during World War I."
- "Here's a powerful moment in history — this room is where World War II really began for the U.S."
- "Japanese diplomats had come to negotiate, and we were trying to stay out of the war."
- "We didn't respond to their demands — and that's when Pearl Harbor was bombed."
- "That fateful conversation happened within these very walls."

## 🦅 CORDELL HULL ROOM

### What to Say:
- "You'll know it by the eagle above the door. Cordell Hull was the last Secretary of State to serve in this building — during World War II."
- "Inside are old pictures of the Eisenhower Building when it was surrounded by horses, not security fences."
- "There's a painting over the fireplace showing cars right up close to the building — I love that painting."
- "Look at the 1943 map on the wall — you'll notice Australasia and Africa are quite different than they are now."
- "Also take a look around the entry space — there are printing press images, eagle details everywhere, and beautiful ornate design."

## 🏢 FIRST FLOOR DISPLAY & DOGE OFFICES

### What to Say:
- "As you step out of the elevator, look to your left and ahead. You'll see a display about the Eisenhower Building's history and the original architectural plans."
- "Turn right and look up — there's another dome in the corner of the building here."
- "Then, as you round the corner, you'll see the Doge offices — rooms 183 to 189."
- "Show guests your desk, the overflowing mail, and maybe offer some Hershey Kisses or M&Ms as a parting treat!"

## ✊ CIVIL RIGHTS ROOM

### What to Say:
- "This room honors the civil rights movement."
- "In the first room, you'll find quotes and photos, including one of Martin Luther King Jr. visiting the West Wing."
- "Check out old photos of the White House — including one where the West Wing is on fire."
- "Others show the old greenhouses and major renovations."
